## Artefact 1: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (2024) – Utilitarianism

### Summary  
The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophies most recent entry on utilitarianism explains ethical theory in detail. The entry focuses on the theory's core principle of maximising overall happiness or wellbeing. The article outlines classical utilitarian thinkers such as Bentham and Mill explaining their thinking. The article also discusses modern applications in digital and technological contexts while highlighting strengths of the theory such as its clear decision making framework. It also addresses criticisms of it, such as the potential neglect of minority rights. The updated 2024 version also includes contemporary examples such as including AI decision making, automated systems and data driven technologies while also showing how utilitarian reasoning is increasingly applied to modern ICT dilemmas.

## Artefact 2: YouTube Video (2024) – “Kantian Ethics Explained Simply”

### Summary  
Throughout this youtube video, the idea of Kantian ethics is clearly presented and explained. It explains the focus on duty, moral rules and the importance of treating individuals as ends rather than means that is at the core of the philosophy. The video also applies Kant’s ideas to modern society, such as data privacy, consent and digital surveillance while also contrasting the ethics with outcome based theories and emphasis that actions must be morally justified regardless of consequences. The presenter in the video also uses real world ICT scenarios to illustrate how Kantian principles apply to issues like algorithmic transparency, user autonomy and responsible system design.



### Reflection  
I selected this artefact as Kantian ethics are essential for understanding professional responsibility in ICT and also because the video helped me understand how duty based reasoning applies to situations where user rights and autonomy must be protected even when doing so may reduce efficiency or convenience. The video also further reinforced the workshop discussions and ideas surrounding respecting stakeholders and ensuring that systems don't manipulate or exploit users in any way shape or form.

## Artefact 3: Scholarly Article (2024) – “Virtue Ethics in Digital Technology Design”

### Summary  
This peer reviewed article from 2024 examines how virtue ethics can be applied to the design and development of digital technologies. The authors of the article express their opinions on focusing on character traits such as honesty, fairness, empathy and integrity and how they believe these traits can lead to more responsible ICT practices. The article also provides case studies that involve software development teams, AI designers and cybersecurity professionals to back up their views. These case studies show how virtuous behaviour influences decision making while also highlighting how virtue ethics encourages long term thinking, professional growth and moral awareness. This paper offers a practical framework for integrating virtue‑based reasoning into ICT projects.


### Reflection  
I chose this article as an artefact as virtue ethics allows for a more personal and character focused approach to ethical decision making. This complements the other theories learnt well. This artefact has also helped me to understand how ethical behaviour in ICT is shaped not only by rules and outcomes but also by the values and habits of the professionals. The case studies also helped to make the ideas feel relevant and practical for workplaces.
