# #Artefact 1: Australia's Social Media Minimum Age Legislation

<img src="images/example.jpg" width="350" height="350">

## #Summary
Artefact 1 is the 2024 Online Safety Amendment Act. This legislation introduces age restrictions for certain social media platforms, as well as requiring providers to take reasonable steps to prevent Australian children under the age of sixteen from holding social media accounts. The legislation was created with the goal of reducing exposure to harmful online content as well as to improve online safety for younger users.
 
### Reflection and Justification
Before examining this legislation, I believed the responsibility for protecting children online rested mainly with parents and technology companies. This artefact helped me understand how governments are increasingly becoming involved in regulating digital platforms. It also helped me further learn how government intervention is often used when existing systems are perceived to be dangerous to vulnerable users. However, the article also helped me to recognise the concerns associated with regulations like these, including privacy, surveillance and freedom of access.
 
I selected this as my first artefact as it demonstrates a very current example of government influence over technology as well as because of how the legislation further highlights how difficult it can be to balance between protecting children and preserving freedom online. It also helped to show how ICT professionals may be required to implement age-verification systems and other compliance measures which could be beneficial in my future career path. The artefact also helped me appreciate that technological decisions often have ethical and social consequences beyond their technical implementation.

# #Artefact 2: Freedom on the Net 2024 Report
 
<img src="images/example.jpg" width="350" height="350">

## #Summary
This 2024 Freedom on the internet report examines internet freedom across multiple countries while also identifying declining online freedom, censorship, election interference, internet shutdowns and government control of online information. The article also highlights how governments are increasingly influencing what citizens can access and share online.
 
## Reflection and Justification
This artefact helped me to significantly change my perspective on censorship, as before reading this report I associated my censorship primarily with authoritarian governments. However, after reading, I learned that digital restrictions exist in many parts of the world and can affect public participation, access to information and democratic processes. While reading the report, one finding that stood out to me was the reports discussion of governments using censorship and information manipulation during elections which can influence public understanding and decision making.

I chose to use this as one of my artefacts as it demonstrates why censorship is not simply a national issue but a global ICT challenge. Technology has transformed how people obtain information which now means governments have new ways to influence what citizens see and discuss online. This artefact also reinforced the idea behind the importance of protecting access to information while still addressing legitimate security concerns. Furthermore, it highlighted the ethical responsibility of ICT professionals who develop and manage digital communication systems.

# #Artefact 3: eSafety Industry Regulation Framework

<img src="images/example.jpg" width="350" height="350">
 
## #Summary
This artefact is the Australian eSafety Commissioner's industry regulation framework from 2025. The framework outlines the powers provided under the Online Safety Act and describes how online service providers are expected to reduce harmful content, respond to complaints, protect children and improve online safety. The framework demonstrates how government agencies work directly with technology companies to regulate online behaviour and digital platforms. 

## #Reflection and Justification
I chose this artefact as it helped me to understand the practical side of censorship and government governance. Before reviewing this resource I had assumed that governments mainly created laws and left implementation entirely to technology companies. Yet after review, I learned that regulatory agencies such as eSafety actively monitor compliance and work directly with digital platforms to enforce standards and address online harms. 

Furthermore, I selected this artefact as it demonstrates how governance operates in realworld ICT environments. It shows the framework that censorship and moderation are not purely technical processes instead they involve ethical decision making, legal requirements and social expectations. This artefact also helped me to understand that ICT professionals must design systems that not only function effectively but also comply with evolving regulatory frameworks while respecting users rights and freedoms.

# #Artefact 4: Workshop Discussion on Censorship and Personal Responsibility

<img src="images/example.jpg" width="350" height="350">
 
## #Summary
During the workshop we discussed censorship, government regulation, parental controls as well as age verification systems and freedom of expression. Several of my classmates argued that governments are increasingly expected to regulate behaviour that individuals could address through personal responsibility and education, with one comment suggesting that education is more effective than censorship because banning content may increase curiosity and encourage individuals to seek information elsewhere.
 
## #Reflection and Justification
This workshop discussion was definitely the most valuable learning experience for me this week because it exposed me to new perspectives from fellow students that challenged my own assumptions. For example, before the discussion I largely viewed censorship as a reasonable solution to harmful content online. Yet after listening to the conversations during the workshop, I found that censorship may not always address the underlying problem. The comment that "education beats censorship" particularly stood out to me because it suggested that digital literacy may be more sustainable than simply restricting access.

I chose this week's workshop as my final artefact because it demonstrated my active engagement with the workshop and shows how my thinking developed through discussion. I also chose this workshop as after listening to other students' opinions, I started to understand that there are no simple answers when balancing safety, freedom of expression, accountability and government intervention. Finally, this discussion also helped to reinforce the importance of considering social impacts rather than focusing solely on technical solutions. As a future ICT professional, I now better understand the need to evaluate both the intended and unintended consequences of policies that restrict information.
